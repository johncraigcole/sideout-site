# Keeping the Camera Phone Cool

Recording a whole game, and especially streaming it at the same time, is about the hardest work you can give an iPhone. Phones have no fan, so heat builds up. This guide explains where the heat comes from, what you can do about it, and what Sideout does to help.

## What makes the heat

Roughly from biggest to smallest:

1. **Fast charging.** Charging warms the battery, which sits right beside the chip that's busy with the video. A fast charger (18 W or more) while streaming is the single biggest extra load.
2. **Streaming over cellular.** Sending a steady stream uploads all game long. In a gym with weak cellular signal, the phone's radio works hard to keep up and gets hot.
3. **Recording quality, especially frame rate.** 60 fps is twice as many pictures per second as 30 fps, so twice the work. 4K is about four times the work of 1080p.
4. **The screen.** A bright screen left on for two hours adds a lot. Sideout dims it for you when the remote is connected (see below).
5. **Sun, a warm gym and a thick case.** These don't make heat, but they keep the phone from getting rid of it.

## Why a 720p stream doesn't make the recording smaller

While you're live, the camera phone makes **two videos at the same time**:

| | The recording | The stream |
|---|---|---|
| Kept where | On the phone, as each set's video | Sent live to viewers |
| Quality | Game Setup ▸ Video (e.g. 1080p, 60 fps) | Live Streaming ▸ Quality (720p or 1080p), always 30 fps |
| Made how | Every camera picture gets the scoreboard and is saved | A copy of each picture is shrunk to the stream size and compressed again |

The camera captures once, at the **recording** quality, and the stream is made from that. So choosing a 720p stream only makes the second video smaller: the phone still does the full recording work as well. That's also why the two can't simply share one video. The recording is kept at high quality, while the stream is compressed much harder, changes with your signal, and shows cards ("Waiting for Set 2", "Stream paused") that never go into your recording.

Keep the stream at **720p**. A 1080p stream adds work and sends more data over cellular, so the phone runs hotter.

## 30 or 60 fps?

Both look the same when you watch at normal speed. Live viewers always see 30 fps.

- **60 fps** gives smoother **slow motion**: a spike or a dig slowed down to half speed still looks smooth, because there are twice as many pictures to stretch out.
- **30 fps** is about half the recording work, so the phone stays noticeably cooler. Slowed down, motion looks choppier. Sharpness is the same: 1080p is 1080p at either frame rate, so jersey numbers and faces look just as clear.

To decide for yourself, record a minute of warm-up at 1080p 60 fps, switch Game Setup ▸ Video to **1080p 30 fps** and record another minute, then play both in **Games**: once at normal speed, and once scrubbing slowly through a hit. If the 30 fps clip looks fine to you, use it for streamed games.

## Before a long game or a tournament

- **Start at 100%.**
- **If you need to charge during the game, use a slow charger:** a small 5 W cube or a basic battery pack, not a fast charger. Or plug in only between sets.
- **Stream over Wi-Fi if you can:** the school's Wi-Fi, or a **Personal Hotspot** from another phone (for example the phone you score on). The camera phone joins it over Wi-Fi and the other phone's cellular radio does the hard work, and takes that heat.
- **Take off a thick case**, and keep the phone out of direct sun and away from hot lights.
- **Consider 1080p 30 fps** for games you stream (see above).
- **Turn Stabilization off** in Game Setup ▸ Video when the phone is on a tripod: it adds work and the tripod doesn't need it.
- **Keep the remote connected**, so the camera phone's screen can dim.

## What Sideout does to help

### Screen dimming

While the remote is connected, the camera phone's screen goes black and its brightness drops right down 20 seconds after it was last touched. Recording, streaming and scoring carry on; a faint "Screen dimmed to keep the phone cool" note shows with REC / LIVE, and a tap wakes it. It wakes by itself if the remote disconnects. Turn it off in Game Setup ▸ **Dim screen while the remote is connected**.

### Heat warnings on both phones

While the camera screen is open, Sideout watches the camera phone's heat and warns on **both phones**:

- **Phone is hot** (orange): the phone is working at its limit. iOS may start slowing things down to protect itself, but recording and streaming carry on.
- **Phone is very hot** (red): iOS may stop the camera soon. If the camera does stop, everything recorded until then is kept.

Staying at **Hot** for a whole game, without reaching Very hot, means the phone is coping, just with no room to spare. A sunny window or a warmer gym could tip it over, so it's worth trying the steps above.

### Pause Stream to Cool Down

When the phone is hot and you're live, the warning has **Pause Stream to Cool Down…** (on either phone). Streaming is the biggest extra load, so pausing it is the quickest way to cool the phone, and the recording keeps going. You can add a message for viewers (up to 60 characters, e.g. "Back for Set 3"). For 30 seconds viewers then see **Stream paused: the camera is too hot**, "Please keep this window open. We'll be back once the phone cools down." and your message; then the stream stops sending. On YouTube the broadcast stays open, so viewers who keep the window open see it come back on the same link when you tap **Resume Stream** (either phone). On Facebook or with a stream key, the live video may end while paused, so the card tells viewers to look for a new live video. **End Stream** ends it for good.

On a small iPhone, the warning can push Pause and Stop below the bottom of SideoutTally's screen: swipe up to reach them.

### Camera Phone Status

From the remote, **⋯ ▸ Camera Phone Status** shows the camera phone's heat, battery, Low Power Mode and recording space, as last reported. Opening it doesn't ask the camera phone for anything, so it adds no extra work.

### After the game

Each set in **Games** notes it if the phone got hot while recording ("Phone got hot at 34:10"). After a tournament you can see when it happened and compare: did it get hot only while streaming, or only while charging?
